## 1. Problem / Opportunity

**Headline:** Retailers plan 12 weeks out and react in days. Reality moves in hours.

**What the audience needs to feel:** the current way is archaic, slow, manual, and leaves money on the floor.

**Talking points:**
- Long-range plans are locked in months ahead — based on last year's data.
- Day-of reactions are panic-driven: print labels Sunday night, hope it lands.
- A category manager owns thousands of SKUs. They can only consciously think about a handful.
- Two failure modes, both expensive:
  - **Priced too low / under-stocked** → sells out, lost revenue, empty shelves.
  - **Priced too high / over-stocked** → meat goes off, waste, forced clearance.

**Concrete pain image:** Barbecue weekend. Manager guesses national demand on Wednesday. Dublin sells out Saturday lunchtime. Cork bins 180 kg of mince Sunday night.

---

## 2. Insight / Background

**Headline:** The current system is humans applying conscious thought, one SKU at a time.

**Talking points:**
- Pricing today: log into a portal, you're not alone in the system, change one product, log out.
- Allocation today: weekly plan, geographic averages, no per-store reactivity.
- Even the best category managers can only look after a small slice of their range.
- The *now* — what's on shelf this hour, what the weather did this morning, what trended on TikTok last night — is mostly uncontrolled.

---

## 3. Why now

**Headline:** Agents can finally reason. The "now" is finally controllable.

**Talking points:**
- Reasoning models changed what's possible: an agent can read inventory, weather, social, sales velocity, and *decide*.
- Retailers already have the data plumbing — POS, inventory, ecomm, weather feeds. We don't need to build a new data platform; we plug in.
- The cost of a human decision has barely moved in 30 years. The cost of a good machine decision just collapsed.
- This shift is happening across every commercial function. Retail is the highest-volume, highest-margin-leverage place to apply it first.

---

## 4. Solution — *(demo slice 1)*

**Headline:** Joey is the retailer's agent. It controls the now.

**Demo moment (live):** Open Joey. *"Monday morning. Joey has been monitoring overnight. It's flagged a barbecue weekend with a Dublin–Cork weather divergence and drafted three actions."* Show the recommendation card.

**Talking points around the demo:**
- Weather is just *one* signal. Joey blends weather + sales history + inventory + lifestyle/social + events.
- The output isn't a dashboard. It's a **decision** — reallocate 400 packs Cork → Dublin, raise Dublin price 5.6%, drop Cork price 8.8%.
- Manager stays in the loop: approve, modify, reject, or "show me why".

## 5. How it works — *(demo slice 2)*

**Headline:** Reasoning over every signal, every SKU, every store, every hour.

**Demo moment (live):** Click "Show me why". Walk through the evidence panel — forecast confidence, historical comparable weekends, days-of-cover.

**Talking points around the demo:**

- Geographic granularity: per-store, not per-region.
- Self-improving: every approval, modification, and rejection is feedback. Joey gets sharper week by week.
- We integrate at the website / POS layer — no new infrastructure required from the retailer.

## 6. Business model

**Headline:** Performance-based. We only win if they win.

**Talking points:**
- **Pilot:** we deploy on a category. We prove uplift against a control. No upfront platform fee.
- **Roll-out:** we take a share of the proven uplift (revenue, margin, waste reduction).
- **Scale formula:** *value captured = total category revenue × % we touch × % uplift × our share*. Plenty of room.
- **Adjacent revenue:** brands and manufacturers will pay for placement and promo signal. Second revenue line, opt-in.

**Risk to address head-on:** Uber's surge pricing got gamed (drivers turning off apps). We design for adversarial behaviour from day one — guardrails, audit trail, human approval on outliers.

## Star metrics to land

- **For the retailer:** revenue uplift, margin uplift, waste reduction, sell-through %.
- **For us internally:** decisions per day, price changes per day, % of SKUs under agent control. Revenue/margin is the north star, but decision velocity is the leading indicator.
